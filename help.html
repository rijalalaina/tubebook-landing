Generating an ebook

Choosing a length

  • Short lead magnet — roughly 10-25 printed pages (~6,000 words, 5 chapters). Great for newsletter freebies and opt-ins. 30 credits.
  • Short book — roughly 25-50 pages (~13,000 words, 9 chapters). Long enough to clear Amazon KDP's 24-page paperback minimum, so you can sell it in print. 60 credits.
  • Full commercial book — long-form, 10-14 chapters, 25,000+ words. Deepest structure and styling. 100 credits.

Every length is available on every plan including Free — they are limited by your credit balance, not by your subscription.

💡 Page counts are estimates from word count and trim size, so the same book shows fewer pages on a larger trim. If you plan to sell a paperback, keep an eye on the page estimate on the generate page: Amazon will not print under 24 pages. A short book at 6″ × 9″ comfortably clears it; on 8.5″ × 11″ it may not.

Fidelity modes

  • High Fidelity — follows the exact structure, names, and ideas of your source. For repurposing your own content only.
  • Smart Adapt (default) — rewrites and restructures the source into fresh text, staying close to the original theme.
  • Low Fidelity — uses the video as a thematic seed only; generates substantially original content safe for commercial publishing (Amazon KDP, Gumroad, etc.).

Writing styles

Choose from Professional, Conversational, Storytelling, Academic, Inspirational, or Stoic before generation begins.

Sources

On the generate page you first choose One source or Many sources.

One source is the classic flow — a single YouTube URL, or a transcript you paste in.

Many sources lets you combine a whole research set into one book. Add as many entries as your plan allows, in any mix:

  • YouTube URL — TubeBook ingests the video directly and pulls its transcript.
  • Web page — paste an article or blog post URL and TubeBook extracts the readable text. Paywalled and JavaScript-only pages can't be read; paste the text instead.
  • Pasted text — any transcript, article, or set of notes (200 characters minimum).
  • Document — a .docx, .pptx or .pdf, either uploaded from your computer (up to 25 MB) or linked by URL. Word manuscripts, slide decks, research papers, reports and course notes all work.

You pick Document and nothing else — TubeBook identifies the format from the file itself, not from its name, so a .docx that is really a PDF is read as the PDF it is instead of failing later.

💡 A scanned PDF — pages that are photographs of text, with nothing selectable — is refused with a clear reason rather than ingested as an empty source. Export a fresh PDF from the original file, or paste the text.

A document costs exactly what a web page costs: no YouTube transcript credit, no search call. Parsing happens inside TubeBook.

Give each source an optional label so you can recognise it later. Sources are numbered S1, S2, S3… in the order you add them.

How multi-source books are written

  1. TubeBook reads every source in turn and summarises each one. You'll see "Reading source 2 of 6" on the dashboard.
  2. It merges those summaries and drafts a single outline across the whole set.
  3. Each chapter is tagged with the sources it draws on — you'll see chips like S1, S4 next to it in the outline review.
  4. When writing, each chapter is given the full text of just its own sources, so it works from real detail rather than a shared summary.
💡 Sources are best-effort. If one URL is dead or paywalled it's skipped and the book is still built from the rest — you'll see "1 of 6 sources couldn't be read" on the dashboard. Your ebook only fails if none of them could be read, and then credits are refunded automatically.

How many sources can I use? Free up to 3, Starter up to 10, Pro up to 25 per ebook.

Pasting a whole manuscript

The paste box takes up to 850,000 characters — about 500 printed pages, so you can drop in an entire unpublished book and have TubeBook restructure it.

Past roughly 47 pages, no single step can read every word at once, so TubeBook works from excerpts — and it is worth knowing exactly how, because it is not "the first fifty pages":

  • The outline is planned from excerpts spread evenly across the whole text, pinned to your real opening and your real ending.
  • Each chapter is written from the stretch of the manuscript it covers, with the windows overlapping so nothing falls between two chapters.

The generate page tells you, before you spend a credit, what proportion the length you picked will reach — for example "At 5 chapters this format reaches about 47% of it." A 200-page manuscript is covered completely at any length; a 500-page one needs a longer format, or split it across several sources by part.

Images

An AI cover is generated on every plan. Per-chapter illustrations (0–5 per chapter) are available on Starter and Pro, at 1 credit per image. More images means a longer generation.

You are not stuck with what comes out — see Cover & illustrations for choosing between options, uploading your own cover, and briefing the artist.

💡 For best quality, review and edit the AI-drafted outline before approving it — chapter scope drives the whole book, and nothing is written until you approve.

Design & readability

Text size

Pick a reading comfort before you generate, or change it on a finished book and rebuild:

  • Standard — 12 pt. The normal setting for a trade paperback.
  • Comfortable — 14 pt. Easier on the eye over a long sitting.
  • Large print — 16 pt. The size large-print editions are actually set at. If your readers are older, this is the one.
  • Custom — anything from 11 to 20 pt.

In a large-print book the front matter keeps its own scale: the copyright notice and the detailed contents stay at 12 pt, because a copyright page set at 16 pt looks like a mistake rather than a kindness.

⚠️ Text size changes your page count, and page count drives the spine width on a paperback wrap. Change it on a book already listed on KDP and you must re-upload both the interior and the cover — the old cover will no longer fit.

Fonts and trim

Choose separate fonts for headings, subtitle and body from twelve families, eight KDP trim sizes from 5″ × 8″ to 8.5″ × 11″, portrait or landscape, and white or cream paper. Cream is slightly thicker, which affects the spine.

Page estimates on the generate page update live as you change any of these, and tell you whether the book clears Amazon's 24-page paperback minimum.

Cover & illustrations

Everything here lives under Edit this book on a finished ebook, and all of it follows one rule: drawing pictures rebuilds nothing. You draw, you compare, you choose — and only then are the files rebuilt, once.

Choosing a cover

Draw 3 options gives you three covers side by side. They stay on a shortlist, so the one you liked four attempts ago is still there. When you press Use this cover, TubeBook redraws the cover page, the Kindle cover and the paperback wrap and rebuilds your files — your chapters and illustrations are untouched.

Three options per press, one per idea rather than three renderings of one idea. If you press again you get three fresh ideas, not the same three redrawn.

💡 The old Regenerate cover button still exists and still works. It draws one picture and rebuilds everything immediately — quicker if you just want a reroll, slower if you want a choice.

Uploading your own cover

Have a designer's artwork, or a photograph you own? Upload your own puts it straight into the shortlist alongside the drawn options.

Upload the picture, not a finished cover. Your title, subtitle and author name are drawn on top afterwards, sized correctly for each format — a cover that already carries its title ends up with the title on it twice. Portrait, at least 1200 × 1800px (ideally 1600 × 2560), PNG, JPEG or WebP, up to 15 MB.

The art brief

TubeBook writes its own art direction from your title, subject and chapter list. That is a reasonable guess and it is only a guess — you may want ink line drawings, a period, a palette, or no people at all. The art brief is where you say so, in your own words:

"Fine ink line drawings, warm sepia, no photographs. Show real people in everyday settings."

One brief covers both the cover and the chapter illustrations, on purpose — a book whose cover is a woodcut and whose chapters are watercolours looks worse than either choice made consistently. It applies to the next cover or illustration you draw, so save it first, then draw.

💡 The brief cannot ask for lettering. Artwork is deliberately wordless — your real title is composed on top afterwards — so a request for a title or a caption in the picture is expressed through imagery instead. It saves you a cover with the title on it twice, once in garbled AI text.

Reviewing chapter illustrations

Review chapter illustrations shows every chapter with its pictures. For each one you can:

  • Untick any that miss. A chapter can have none at all if you prefer.
  • Draw 2 more for that chapter alone — the other chapters are untouched, so the eleven you already accepted stay exactly as they are.

A redraw keeps the same subject and moves the camera — wider, closer, lower, tighter on the hands — because you objected to the picture, not to what the chapter is about.

Selections save as you make them. Nothing reaches your files until you press Save & rebuild files, so one rebuild covers a whole review pass however many chapters you adjusted.

What the pictures are actually of

Before anything is drawn, TubeBook turns your book into nameable physical scenes — one for the cover, one per chapter. It is the difference between a picture of your subject and a picture of a mood. A book about conversation gets people mid-conversation; a book about a craft gets the tools and the workspace.

Where the facts come from

This is the setting that decides whether you can stand behind your own book, and it is easy to confuse with fidelity. They answer different questions:

  • Fidelity governs expression — the structure, the ordering, the wording, which examples appear.
  • Grounding governs substance — which facts, figures, names and claims may appear at all.

TubeBook never searches the web while writing your book. There is no retrieval step, no live lookup, nothing added from the internet once generation starts. But that is not the same promise as "only your sources": a large AI model carries an enormous amount of subject knowledge, and left unconstrained it will happily supply a statistic, a date or a piece of background you never provided. So you choose:

  • My sources only (default) — every fact, figure, name, quotation and example must come from the material you supplied. If the model knows something relevant that your sources do not contain, it is left out. Depth comes from working your material harder, not from importing new material.
  • Sources + AI knowledge — your sources stay the backbone, and the AI may add well-established background where it genuinely helps. Books built this way are badged on your dashboard so you can spot them later.

Neither setting may invent evidence. On both, the model is forbidden from inventing statistics, studies, citations or quotations, and from attributing anything to a named person or organisation that your sources do not. "Sources + AI knowledge" means add background, never add evidence.

💡 Why My sources only is the default: it is the only setting you can truly vouch for. Everything in the book traces back to something you chose. If you switch to the other one, fact-check anything you cannot trace to a source before you publish.

Combine My sources only with High Fidelity and you have a genuine "restructure my own manuscript" mode — your material is the only thing allowed in.

One nuance worth stating, because the two settings look like they collide: on Low Fidelity with My sources only, the AI writes original prose and original illustrations of your points, but it may not invent facts to fill them. An invented number is invented whether or not it replaced a real one.

Choosing what goes in your book

When you review the outline you also get a checklist of the book's parts. Chapters are always included; everything else is yours to choose:

  • Title page, Copyright page, Foreword, Table of contents, Detailed contents
  • Introduction and Conclusion
  • Call to action, About the author, Contact / back cover
💡 Untick the introduction or conclusion at the outline stage and they are never written, so you do not pay to generate them. Untick them later and they are only left out of the files.

How each chapter ends

By default every chapter closes with a Key Takeaways list and a short Reflection prompt. Both are now yours to switch off — separately — for a book that simply reads as prose.

Unlike the parts above, these can be removed from a book that is already written: they are taken out when the files are built, so unticking one and rebuilding is free and rewrites nothing. Tick it again and it comes back.

💡 If you ever saw a chapter with a "Key Takeaways" heading and nothing under it, that is fixed: any heading with no content beneath it is now dropped from the files, whatever it says.

Changing your mind later

On any finished book, use Change what's in this book to adjust the selection and rebuild the files. This is free and costs no credits — these parts are assembled when the files are built, so nothing is rewritten.

The copyright page

Fill in as much or as little as you want: pen name, publisher or imprint, city and country, edition, cover designer, interior designer, and country of printing. Anything left blank is omitted entirely — no "[Publisher Name]" ever prints in your book.

The optional disclaimer states that the book is educational and that neither author nor publisher is providing legal, financial or medical advice. It is on by default.

Set these once under Profile → Imprint defaults and every new book pre-fills from them. Each book keeps its own copy, so editing your profile never changes a book you have already published.

The foreword

A foreword is written by someone else — a peer, mentor or recognised voice in your field. Give their name, credentials, location and date, then either write the text yourself or have TubeBook draft it from your book's real topic and chapters.

💡 If you use the drafted version, have the person named on it read and approve it before you publish. It carries their name, not yours.

QR codes

You can add a QR code to the About the author page, pointing at your website, your Amazon book page, or anywhere else. It is printed in the paperback only — in a Kindle or EPUB edition a reader taps a link instead, so it would just take up space.

One thing worth knowing about Amazon. KDP's hyperlink guidelines prohibit "links to web forms that request customer information" inside a book — which is exactly what an email opt-in or lead-magnet landing page is. Links to Amazon itself are fine, and links to other books in your series are explicitly permitted. A QR code is an image rather than a link, so it is not covered by that rule in so many words, but Amazon reserves the right to remove links at its discretion. TubeBook warns you when a QR target looks like a sign-up page, and prints it anyway if you keep it — it is your book.

Title & subtitle

Amazon's 200-character limit

KDP allows 200 characters for the title and subtitle combined, spaces and punctuation included. Go over and Amazon rejects the listing. TubeBook counts them for you wherever they can be edited, and warns before you are over rather than after.

Editing them after the book is built

Under Edit this book you can rewrite the title, subtitle and author name on a finished ebook and rebuild. This is free. The cover artwork is kept — only the lettering over it is redrawn — and the Amazon description and keywords are rewritten to match the new title.

AI title suggestions

Suggest SEO titles proposes alternatives written against your book's actual chapters and, if the book came from Find a topic, the phrases readers really searched for. Each suggestion shows its character count against the 200 limit. Clicking one fills the fields; nothing is saved until you press save, so trying them costs nothing.

Suggestions are written to work three ways at once: search (keywords people actually type), answer engines (a subtitle that reads as a direct, literal answer), and AI recommendations (a title and subtitle that make the subject, scope and reader unmistakable when read on their own, with no cover around them). And never keyword-stuffed — a title that reads as SEO bait loses the human sale and the AI recommendation together.

Your writing style

On your Profile page, under Your writing style, upload something you have already written — a blog post, a chapter, an essay — or paste a few pages in. Your books are then written the way you write, rather than in the careful, even register that AI writing falls into by default.

What actually happens

Your sample is read once and a description of how you write is worked out from it: sentence length and how much it varies, whether you address the reader directly, how you open a section and how you close one, your punctuation habits, what you never do. That description is shown to you, along with a few short observations, so you can judge whether it got you right.

Every chapter of every book you generate afterwards is written with that description in front of the writer. It replaces the generic tone setting, which was previously a single sentence — that is the whole reason it makes a visible difference.

🔒 This is not model training, and your writing is yours. No model is fine-tuned, nothing you upload is used for anyone else’s books, and your files are deleted as soon as they have been read. Only the description and short excerpts are kept — the excerpts so the description can be worked out again when you add another sample. Teaching us how you write should not hand us a permanent copy of your manuscript.

What it will and will not do

It shifts register, rhythm and vocabulary, and it is the single biggest lever on how a generated book reads. It will not make the writing indistinguishable from yours, and we would rather say so than let you find out. A few pages works far better than a few paragraphs: one paragraph is an accident, a few pages is a style.

💡 Your style is copied onto each book when you create it. Refining your profile later changes future books and never rewrites one you have already published. At High Fidelity it is ignored, exactly as the per-book tone setting already is — that mode reproduces the speaker’s own voice from the source, and a personal writing manner on top would defeat the point.

Editing & proofreading

On any finished book, Edit the writing opens the editor: your chapters down the left, the text of one of them beside it. A finished book is not a frozen one.

Change anything yourself

Edit a chapter and press Save. That is free and unlimited — typing is not a service we charge for.

📄 Your files keep working while you edit. The PDF, EPUB, DOCX and PPTX you already have stay downloadable and keep the previous wording until you press Rebuild files. We could have deleted them the moment you changed a word, but taking your downloads away because you fixed a typo is worse than telling you plainly that they are a version behind.

Have a section rewritten

Under the text is a list of the chapter’s sections. Pick one, optionally say what should change (“tighten it”, “add a concrete example”), and it comes back rewritten — in the same style, fidelity, grounding and language as the rest of your book, having read the sections either side so it joins up. That last part is why it does not read as a foreign paragraph dropped into your chapter.

1 credit per rewrite. It is charged before the model runs and refunded automatically if anything fails, so you never pay for prose you did not receive.

Proofread the manuscript

Check reads the chapter you are on. Check the whole book reads every chapter and then looks for the problems no single chapter can show you: the same idea given two different names, a term used before it is introduced, a promise made and never kept, two chapters covering the same ground.

Findings are listed worst first. Each one quotes the exact words it objects to, and clicking it selects those words in the editor.

💡 Why some real problems are not reported. Every finding has to quote your text exactly, and we search for that quote in the chapter before showing it. Anything we cannot find is thrown away — including, occasionally, a genuine problem the model quoted slightly wrong. That is deliberate. A list where every item can be trusted is more useful than a longer list you have to verify yourself, which was the job the check was supposed to do.

Checking is free, however many times you run it. Findings are not saved: a check describes your text at that moment, and your next edit would leave it pointing at sentences that no longer exist.

Ask for a second opinion

Under the chapter is Ask about this chapter. It has read your outline and the chapter you are on, so it can answer questions about direction, pacing, whether a chapter earns its length, or what a weak opening is missing. It will tell you when something is already fine, and it will disagree with you once if it thinks a change would weaken the book.

Ask it for a change to the wording and it comes back as a proposal: the full replacement text, which section it would replace, and Accept or Discard. It never edits your book on its own — accepting is the same action as saving your own typing, so everything that follows an edit (files marked out of date, narration marked out of step) happens exactly the same way.

Free, and the conversation is not saved — it belongs to the sitting you are in.

What editing affects

  • Your files — a version behind until you rebuild, as above.
  • Your audiobook — if you edit a chapter that has been narrated, that part is marked as no longer matching. Nothing is deleted: you spent audio credits on it, and a typo fix must not destroy something you paid for. Narrate again when you want them back in step.
  • Nothing else. Your cover, illustrations, translations and KDP metadata are untouched.

References

Tick References in Book parts and your book gains a source list at the back, grouped by the chapter that used each source. Rebuild and it appears — including on a book you finished weeks ago.

It is built from the material actually fed to the writer: the videos, articles and documents you supplied, and the record of which chapters were written from which. It is not generated by asking a model what it thinks it read, which would produce plausible-looking citations that may not correspond to anything. For a research-backed book that is worse than having no bibliography at all.

📚 Off by default, on purpose. Every book made before this existed would otherwise grow a bibliography the next time its files were rebuilt — including ones already on sale. If you tick it and nothing is listed, your book was written from a single pasted transcript with nothing to cite, and we leave the section out rather than print an empty heading.

Publishing in another language

On any finished book, Edit this book → Publish in another language creates a full edition in French, Spanish, Portuguese, German, Italian, Dutch or English. Every one of them can also be narrated, with a male or female voice, so a translated edition can ship as an audiobook exactly like the original — see Audiobooks.

It becomes a separate book

Not extra files on the original — its own book on your dashboard, because that is what Amazon needs. Every language is its own KDP listing with its own ASIN and its own keywords, so the French edition gets its own cover lettering, its own Amazon description, its own files and its own edit controls. The original is untouched and the two are linked, so you can find one from the other.

Why it costs a fraction of the book

Your cover artwork and every chapter illustration are reused — copied into the new edition, so it keeps working even if you later delete the original. Only the words are redone, which is the cheap half. That is why a translation is about 40% of what the book cost: 12 credits for a lead magnet, 24 for a short book, 40 for a commercial one.

What is translated, and what is rewritten

  • Translated — every chapter, the title, the subtitle and the chapter list, as a book for readers in that language rather than word for word. Idioms become that language's idioms; proper nouns, brand names, numbers and statistics stay exactly as they are.
  • Rewritten, not translated — the Amazon description and the 7 KDP keywords, generated fresh for that market. German readers do not search translated English phrases, and keywords are the whole point of that pack.
  • Redrawn — the cover lettering, in the new language, over the same artwork.
💡 Only these seven languages are offered, and the reason is worth knowing: the copyright page, the contents and the section headings are properly localised for exactly these. A book whose chapters are in one language and whose front matter is in English is visibly half-translated, which is worse than not offering it. More will be added as those are localised.

Audiobooks

Edit this book → Make an audiobook narrates a finished book. It runs after your files exist, so the book stays downloadable throughout and a narration problem never affects the ebook you already paid for.

The voices

  • Standard — male and Standard — female — clear and consistent, and audibly synthetic. English, French, Spanish, Portuguese, German, Italian, Dutch, Chinese, Japanese and Korean. 40 / 80 / 120 audio credits by book length.
  • Premium — natural, expressive narration, with Thalia, Apollo (English) and Celeste (Spanish). English and Spanish only — the premium engine publishes no model for the other eight, so a German or Japanese book uses a standard voice. 100 / 200 / 300 audio credits.

Both standard voices cover all ten narration languages — English, French, Spanish, Portuguese, German, Italian, Dutch, Chinese, Japanese and Korean — and each one keeps the same narrator sex across every language, so a book published in several languages does not change narrator between editions. Every language you can publish in can also be narrated; Chinese, Japanese and Korean are narration-only, for books already written in them.

🎧 Audiobooks are billed from a separate audio-credit balance, not from your ebook credits. Writing a book and narrating one cost us wildly different amounts, so keeping the two apart is what lets ebook credits stay cheap. Audio credits come with the Pro Audio plan, or can be bought outright on any plan — see below.

Only voices that can actually speak your book's language are offered, and the panel says so rather than quietly showing a shorter list.

💡 Premium costs more than the ebook it narrates, and that is not a markup — narrating a commercial book with the premium engine genuinely costs us more than the book earns. Standard costs us a few pence, which is why it is cheap. Try Standard first; if you are selling the audiobook, Premium is worth it.

One file per part — and one combined file

You get a file per part, with a player on each so you can skip to the one you want to check. Parts are stored as lossless WAV masters at the rate the narration engine actually produces, and each one can be downloaded from the link under its player. Then tick the parts you want and Download as one audiobook joins them, in book order, into a single MP3 at 192 kbps. Nothing is re-narrated and no credits are spent — you already paid for those parts.

The MP3s are built in your browser when you click, which is why a long book takes a minute. That is deliberate: encoding from the lossless master means the one lossy step happens once, at the end. Converting a small preview up to 192 kbps would triple the file size while preserving the flaws of the first encode rather than the audio.

Per-part files are what lets us produce a three-hour audiobook at all, and they are what makes the combined file a choice rather than a fixed result: drop the copyright page, keep the call to action, re-narrate one chapter without touching the rest.

The first file opens with your title, subtitle and author name; the last closes with a sign-off. Headings, bullet lists and abbreviations are rewritten for the ear — a list is read as "First… Second… And finally…", "40%" becomes "40 percent", and links and image captions are dropped, because a narrator reading a URL aloud is the fastest way to lose a listener.

Your Key Takeaways and Reflection settings are respected, so the audiobook contains the same words your ebook does. Every part is narrated — including the copyright page, the tables of contents, your call to action and your author bio — because whether an audiobook opens with a copyright line or closes with your offer is your decision, not ours. Chapters are ticked for the combined file by default and the front and back matter is not, so the default is the book alone.

The lossless masters

What the player streams is the master — narration is stored as lossless PCM (WAV), and nothing is transcoded for playback. Under each player is a link showing its size, e.g. Lossless master · WAV 24 kHz, 34 MB, which downloads that part named for your book and chapter rather than ch03.wav.

They are kept on the same 30-day clock as the book, and they are not a Pro Audio extra — any narrated book has them, including one narrated with a single audio credit pack on the free plan.

🎚️ The masters are 24 kHz, not 44.1 kHz, because that is what the narration engines actually produce — every one of them is natively 24 kHz. The 44.1 kHz in the packaged MP3s is a resample done at encode time to meet the container spec. Storing the archive at 44.1 would double its size and add nothing: it would be an upsampled 24 kHz file wearing a larger number.

The distribution package

Download package (ZIP) builds everything an aggregator asks for, in one click:

  • Every part as a numbered MP3 — 00-title-page.mp3, 02-chapter-01.mp3 — zero-padded so a plain alphabetical sort is also the right play order, which is how an uploader reads them.
  • 192 kbps CBR, 44.1 kHz, mono, levelled to about −20 dB RMS with peak below −3 dB.
  • cover.jpg at 3000×3000, RGB — the square artwork every audiobook store requires and no ebook cover is.
  • retail-sample.mp3, the first five minutes of chapter one, cut on a frame boundary.
  • metadata.json — title, author, narrator, language, per-file durations and measured levels, and an explicit "narration": "digital".
  • ID3 tags and embedded cover art on every track, so a buyer who unzips into VLC sees a book rather than a dozen anonymous files.
🔊 Why the disclosure is in the file. Most aggregators require AI narration to be declared. A package that quietly omits it is not rejected at upload — it is taken down after distribution, once it has been sold. Declaring it costs nothing and is the difference between a catalogue entry and a takedown.

Selling it — read this before you upload

Selling direct is the simple route — Gumroad, Payhip, Shopify, or a download on your own site. No approval, no audio-spec review, and you keep the margin. The combined single file is the format those stores expect.

Audible and ACX are possible but conditional. ACX's standard submission flow is for human narration: its Audio Submission Requirements say a title must be human-narrated unless otherwise authorized, and list unauthorized text-to-speech and AI as prohibited. That is a rule against unauthorized AI, not a ban on AI — but the difference is one you have to arrange in advance, not discover at submission:

  • Contact ACX support first and get the title authorized, rather than submitting through the standard flow and hoping.
  • Disclose the AI narration in the book description. This is enforced.
  • ACX measures audio levels — RMS between −23 and −18 dB, peak no higher than −3 dB, noise floor under −60 dB.
  • KDP Virtual Voice is not a route for these files. It is Amazon's own AI narration programme, it uses Amazon's voices, and it does not accept audio you supply.

Aggregators are the easier path to Audible. Author's Republic, Kobo Writing Life and Spotify's audiobook programme (formerly Findaway Voices) accept AI-narrated audiobooks and distribute into Audible's catalogue, with AI disclosure required. No per-title authorization to negotiate.

We do now measure and set audio levels. Every file in the package and every combined download is analysed and levelled to roughly −20 dB RMS with peak below −3 dB — the same window listed above — and the measured figures for each track are written into metadata.json so you can check them without opening an editor. Loudness is measured on the speech itself rather than averaged with the pauses between sentences, which is what stops a part with long gaps from being pushed louder than the rest of the book.

We still will not tell you a file is ACX-compliant, because levels are the part we can prove and authorization is not. A file that hits every number in the spec is still ineligible for ACX's standard flow until ACX has authorized the title. The technical side is ours; the permission is yours.

📦 Two different ZIPs. Audio is not in your ebook archive — an audiobook is far too large to sit alongside the PDF and EPUB. The audiobook has its own Download package (ZIP) in the audiobook panel, described above.

Find a topic

Find a topic answers the question that comes before everything else: what book is worth writing? Enter a niche and TubeBook shows you what people are actually searching for, how crowded each topic already is, and a set of book concepts built from the gaps — then finds the sources to write it from.

Starting with no idea at all

If you don't have a niche yet, open the What's trending tab. It shows a board per country — United States, United Kingdom, Germany, Canada, Australia and France — built from trending YouTube how-to and education videos in that country, plus what shoppers type into that country's own Amazon store. Browsing is free; picking a topic runs the normal 5-credit research on it.

Boards are rebuilt weekly and show their date. Two things worth knowing about how to read them:

  • Many Amazon phrases are existing book titles. "How to Win Friends and Influence People" is not a topic — it is evidence that readers in that market want books about social skills. The suggested topics are the underlying subjects, not titles to copy.
  • The "spiking in the news" row is not book demand. It is Google's daily trending searches, which are dominated by sport, share prices and celebrities. It is shown separately, and labelled, because occasionally a real story starts a real book — but most of it is noise.
💡 Country is as precise as this gets. Book demand genuinely varies by city, but no free data source breaks it down that far, so we don't pretend to.

What the numbers mean

This is the important part to understand. The demand figures come from what Amazon and Google autocomplete when someone starts typing — real queries, ordered by how often people type them. They are search-demand signals, not Amazon sales ranks.

We would love to show you true Best Seller Rank, and we deliberately do not fake it. Amazon retired public access to that data: the Product Advertising API closed in May 2026, and its replacement requires an Amazon Associates account with ongoing qualifying sales. So no honest tool can hand you live BSR without you having your own seller relationship with Amazon.

  • Score (0-100) — a ranking aid only, combining demand with competition. Use it to order your shortlist, never as a sales forecast.
  • Book buyers — this phrase autocompletes inside Amazon's Books or Kindle Store, not just all departments. It is the strongest buying signal available here, because the person typing it is already shopping for a book.
  • Competition — how many books already target the phrase and how heavily reviewed they are. A crowded shelf of forgotten titles is easier to enter than a small shelf of bestsellers, so both are weighed.
  • Competition: unknown — the competition lookup was unavailable for that phrase. It means we don't know, not that the topic is wide open.

Suggested sources

Pick a concept and TubeBook shortlists up to 25 sources for it — as many as a Pro book can combine. Filters are applied before you ever see the list:

  • Videos must have captions, because that is what TubeBook reads. A video without them cannot be turned into a chapter, so suggesting it would waste your credits.
  • Videos under four minutes are dropped — too little for a chapter. Long-form talks, lectures and interviews are included, and each row shows the runtime, which is the best single guide to how much of a chapter a video can carry.
  • PDFs and Office documents in the results — papers, government reports, whitepapers, course notes — are recognised as documents and read with the document extractor rather than being handed to a web reader that cannot open them.
  • Every link is checked for reachability and safety first, using the same validation the ingester applies.

The first ten are shown; Show 10 more reveals the rest. Those are already loaded — revealing them costs nothing and makes no new searches.

How the concepts are written

Each proposed title and subtitle has to work for all three ways a book gets found today: search (keywords drawn from phrases really observed, never invented), answer engines (a subtitle that reads as a direct, literal answer — a number, a timeframe, a named situation, and no wordplay), and AI recommendations (a title and subtitle that make the subject, scope and reader unmistakable read on their own). Concepts also respect Amazon's 200-character title-plus-subtitle limit, so none of them arrive unusable.

Better KDP keywords

When a book comes from a researched topic, its 7 KDP keywords are drawn from the phrases actually observed in search rather than invented by the AI. An invented keyword nobody types ranks for nothing, so this is a real difference in discoverability.

The publishing-risk check

Amazon puts the burden on you, the publisher: you must "ensure their content doesn't violate laws or copyright, trademark, brand, privacy, publicity, or other rights." On metadata it goes further and states a zero-tolerance policy for anything meant to advertise, promote or mislead — "including brands that you do not own or that you are unauthorized to use". The usual penalty is that your title stops selling; repeat problems can reach the account itself.

So TubeBook reviews your book's metadata and flags what a reasonable KDP reviewer would act on:

  • Brands — a commercial trademark you don't own (Lego, Notion, Peloton).
  • Franchises and characters — Harry Potter, Pokémon, Star Wars.
  • Living authors and public figures the book trades on by name.
  • Claims of endorsement — "official", "authorised", or a summary or workbook framing that implies permission. Companion works based on an in-copyright book need written permission from the rights holder.
  • Song lyrics and long quotations from in-copyright work.
  • Undifferentiated public-domain reprints, which KDP refuses where a free version already exists.
  • Keywords that don't describe your book — keyword stuffing.

When it runs

Twice. Once when you approve the outline, where the title and subtitle are still yours to edit, and again on the generated KDP metadata — because a perfectly clean title can still have a brand name sitting in one of the 7 keywords, and that is exactly the kind of thing that gets a listing suppressed.

Suspected trademarks are confirmed

Every brand or franchise it flags comes with a one-click link to the US trademark register, so you can see the actual owner and whether the mark is live. "MONOPOLY, registered to Hasbro" tells you far more than "this might be a brand" — and checking takes seconds.

On Pro, you can skip the clicking: one button checks every flagged brand and franchise at once and prints the registered owner, the live/dead status and the serial number beside each finding. It costs 1 credit for the whole book, however many marks were flagged, and you are only asked after you have seen what was found — nothing is checked, or charged, automatically. A book with nothing flagged never offers it.

The per-mark links stay free on every plan, and the USPTO's own search is free to use directly. You are paying for the lookups being done for you and shown in place, not for access to public data.

💡 Why this isn't just a trademark database search: nearly every ordinary English word is a registered trademark in some class. Citadel, Moment, Toolbox, Journal and Living all belong to someone. A tool that matched your title against the register would flag every book ever written, and you would rightly stop reading it. The register is there to confirm a specific suspicion, not to generate them.

What it will not do

It never blocks your book, and it never tells you a book is safe. When it finds nothing it says "nothing flagged" — not "approved" and not "no issues", because those would be promises nobody can make. This is an automated check, not legal advice, it can miss things, and it cannot predict Amazon's decision. If your book touches someone else's rights, clearing that is still your job — and if you are unsure, ask a lawyer rather than a checker.

Troubleshooting

Find a topic returned no results

The suggestion services are occasionally unavailable. Nothing is charged when research fails — the 5 credits are refunded automatically. Try again in a few minutes, or use a broader niche term.

No sources were suggested for my topic

Source discovery draws on shared daily quotas from YouTube and our search provider. When those are used up for the day, TubeBook tells you so rather than silently returning an empty list — your research results are unaffected, and you can add sources by hand on the generate page.

"Not enough transcript capacity this month"

YouTube transcripts come from a shared monthly allowance. When there is not enough left for the videos in your book, TubeBook refuses before charging you and tells you how many remain and when the allowance resets — rather than building the book from whichever sources happened to fit and billing you for it. Non-YouTube sources are unaffected, so a book from documents, articles or pasted text still runs.

My chapter illustrations don't suit the book

Open Edit this book → Review chapter illustrations: untick any that miss, or draw new options for that one chapter. Write an art brief first if you want a particular style, then draw. Nothing is rebuilt until you save.

Generation seems stuck

Generations resume automatically if a step is interrupted — no action needed. Most ebooks complete within a few minutes; long commercial ebooks with images can take longer.

YouTube URL won't ingest

Make sure the video is public and has captions (auto or manual). Private, age-restricted, or live videos cannot be processed.

I don't see my ebook

Open Dashboard. Ebooks are kept for 30 days after generation — a countdown is shown on each card. Download anything you want to keep before it expires. Audiobook narration expires with its book, on the same countdown, so build the package before the clock runs out.

Download link expired

Signed download URLs expire after 24 hours. The app refreshes them on demand — just open your Dashboard and the links will be updated automatically.
